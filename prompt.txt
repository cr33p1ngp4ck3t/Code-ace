SOCIAL NOVA HACKATHON 2026 Challenge Handout: The Digital Shield Date: September 12, 2026 1. Background Context The internet has become dangerously convincing. With the rapid rise of Generative AI, creating hyper-realistic fake images, deep-fake videos, and cloned audio is easier than ever. Simultaneously, digital fraud, such as fake internship offers, phishing links, and financial scams, has flooded social media platforms. While tech-savvy users can often spot the red flags, millions of internet users with limited formal education or low digital literacy are falling victim to these traps every single day. The problem is not just technological; it is a fundamental issue of accessibility and education. 2. The Core Problem Currently, the tools and advice available for spotting fake AI content or online scams require reading complex articles, understanding technical jargon, or navigating complicated verification websites. There is a massive gap in accessibility. The Core Challenge How can we empower everyday, non-technical, or illiterate internet users to easily identify, verify, and flag AI-generated deepfakes and digital scams before they get fooled? 3. The Challenge Task Your challenge is to design an innovative, accessible, and user-friendly solution that bridges this digital divide. The solution must serve as a "Digital Shield" that helps an uneducated or digitally inexperienced user recognize deception. We are looking for solutions that are not just effective, but also intuitive and culturally sensitive for a diverse range of users. 4. Acceptable Solution Formats You are not strictly required to write complex code. Your team can choose to build and present any of the following: a. Software Prototype A functional app, browser extension, or chatbot. b. "Vibe-Coded" / UI Mockup A visually complete, interactive design of a digital tool showing how the user would experience it. c. Physical Project / Hardware A physical model or interactive kiosk concept designed for public spaces. d. Awareness Campaign / Poster Series A highly effective, visually driven, language-independent educational campaign. Note: This must be more than just a poster; it should be a campaign concept with a clear strategy for distribution. 5. Pitch & Demo Requirements Your team's submission will be evaluated in two parts: A. The Pitch (The "Why") Your team will present a slide deck on the main stage. This presentation must clearly explain: 1. Your core idea and the "shielding" mechanism. 2. Your target audience (who is most vulnerable, and why your solution is for them). 3. Why your solution works (how it addresses the problem of accessibility). 4. Your team's strategy for implementation. B. The Demo (The "What") After the pitch, your team must display and demonstrate your actual solution (prototype, model, posters, etc.). This is your chance to show the judges and other participants how your "Digital Shield" operates in the real world. Judging Criteria Your submission will be evaluated on the following criteria: 1. Innovation & Creativity How novel and unique is your solution? 2. Accessibility & Inclusivity How well does it serve the target audience of non-technical or low-literacy users? 3. Feasibility & Impact How practical is your solution to implement, and what is its potential real-world impact? 4. Clarity of Pitch How effectively did you communicate your idea and its value? 5. Quality of Demo How well does the final prototype or model demonstrate the core functionality of your solution?
